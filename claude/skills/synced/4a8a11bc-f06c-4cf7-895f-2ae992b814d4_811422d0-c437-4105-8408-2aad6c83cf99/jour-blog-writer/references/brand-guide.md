# Jour — Brand & Product Reference

Read this file before writing any Jour blog content. It defines the voice, product facts, and positioning rules.

## Product Facts

- **Name:** Jour
- **Domain:** jour.caipora.site
- **Type:** Free, minimalist journaling web app
- **Stack:** Next.js (SSR), Turbopack
- **Price:** Free. No premium tier currently.
- **Platform:** Web only (desktop + mobile browser)

### Core Features (accurate as of April 2026)
- Daily journal entries with rich text editor and auto-save
- Daily habit tracking with a monthly consistency grid
- Monthly goal setting
- Customizable reflection questions (answered monthly)
- Distraction-free, minimalist interface

### What Jour Does NOT Have (do not claim or imply these)
- Native iOS or Android app
- AI-powered prompts or coaching
- Voice journaling / dictation
- Photo, video, or audio attachments
- End-to-end encryption (do not claim unless confirmed)
- Integration with health apps, calendars, or social platforms
- Guided journaling programs or courses
- Import/export tools
- Multiple journal support

Be honest about these gaps when writing comparison content. Jour's strength is simplicity, not feature count.

## Brand Positioning

Jour is the **science-backed, minimalist journaling app** for people who want a calm, focused space to write — not another feature-bloated productivity tool.

**Core positioning statement:**
"A calmer way to start and end your day."

**Target audience:**
- People who want to journal but feel overwhelmed by complex apps
- People interested in the mental health benefits of journaling (backed by research)
- People who value simplicity and focus over features
- People already familiar with journaling who want a quieter tool

**What Jour is NOT:**
- A productivity app
- An AI therapy bot
- A social platform
- A "life operating system"

## Voice & Tone

### Voice Attributes
- **Calm.** Like the app itself — no hype, no urgency, no clickbait.
- **Honest.** We cite research. We don't exaggerate. If a competitor does something better, we say so.
- **Warm but not saccharine.** Friendly without being performatively upbeat.
- **Grounded in evidence.** Every health or mental health claim should be supported by at least one peer-reviewed source.
- **Minimalist in prose, not in depth.** We write clearly and concisely, but we go deep on substance.

### Voice Don'ts
- No "In today's fast-paced world..." or "In an era of..." openings. Cut the preamble.
- No "journaling is a powerful tool that can transform your life" without citing evidence for the specific claim.
- No fake enthusiasm. No "Amazing!" or "Incredible benefits!"
- No condescending tone. Don't talk down to readers.
- No AI-slop tells: "dive into", "unlock your potential", "harness the power", "game-changer", "let's explore", "without further ado", "it's important to note that".
- No "we" unless referring to the Jour team specifically. Use "you" when addressing the reader.
- Don't oversell Jour. Mention it 2-3 times per article, naturally, where it genuinely fits. The article should be valuable even if the reader never uses Jour.

### Voice Examples

**Bad:** "In today's busy world, journaling has emerged as an incredible tool for unlocking your full potential. Let's dive into the amazing benefits!"

**Good:** "Expressive writing reduces anxiety symptoms by an average of 9%, according to a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials [1]. That's not a vague promise — it's a measured outcome from clinical research."

**Bad:** "Jour is the best journaling app available, offering everything you need for your journaling journey."

**Good:** "Jour keeps things simple: a clean editor, daily habit tracking, and monthly goals. If you want AI coaching or multimedia entries, Day One or Reflection might be a better fit. If you want a quiet place to write without distractions, that's what Jour is built for."

## Research & Citation Standards

This is Jour's competitive edge. Every article should demonstrate genuine expertise through citations.

### Citation Rules
- Use numbered inline references: `[1]`, `[2]`, etc. in the text. Each number maps to a full entry in a `## References` section at the bottom of the article.
- Number references sequentially in order of first appearance in the text.
- Always cite the actual study: author(s), journal name, year. Not "studies show" or "research suggests" with no source.
- Use web_search to verify studies are real and the claims attributed to them are accurate. Do NOT fabricate citations.
- Prefer peer-reviewed journal articles. Acceptable sources: journals indexed in PubMed, PsycINFO, or Google Scholar. Also acceptable: books by established researchers (e.g., Pennebaker).
- If you can't verify a study, don't cite it. Being wrong about a citation is worse than not having one.
- In the References section, include DOIs or PubMed URLs where possible.
- Format: `[N] Author(s). "Title." *Journal Name*, Volume(Issue), Pages, Year. DOI/URL.`

### Commonly Cited Studies in This Space (verify details before using)
- Pennebaker & Beall (1986) — Original expressive writing study
- Smyth et al. (1999, JAMA) — Journaling improves health outcomes in chronic illness patients
- Ramirez & Beilock (2011, Science) — Expressive writing before exams improves performance
- Smyth et al. (2018, JMIR Mental Health) — Web-based positive journaling reduces mental distress
- Baikie & Wilhelm (2005) — Review of emotional/physical benefits of expressive writing
- Sohal et al. (2022) — Systematic review and meta-analysis of journaling for mental illness
- Klein & Boals (2001) — Expressive writing improves working memory
- Pennebaker & Chung (2007) — Optimal journaling frequency (3-4x/week)

These are starting points. Search for additional studies relevant to each specific topic.

## SEO & Structural Requirements

### On-Page SEO
- One H1 per article (the title)
- Use H2s for major sections, H3s for subsections
- Include primary keyword in: title, first 100 words, at least one H2, meta description
- Use secondary keywords naturally — don't force them
- Write a unique meta description (140-155 chars) that compels clicks

### Content Structure
- Lead with value. The first 2-3 sentences should tell the reader what they'll get from this article.
- No "Introduction" heading. Just start.
- Use clear, scannable structure: short paragraphs (2-4 sentences), descriptive subheadings
- Include at least one table, list, or structured element that AI systems can easily extract
- End with a brief, non-pushy mention of Jour where appropriate (not every article needs a CTA)

### AI/LLM Optimization
- Structure content so key facts can be extracted as standalone answers
- Include a clear, direct answer near the top for question-based keywords (e.g., "Does journaling help anxiety?" → answer in the first paragraph, then expand)
- Use FAQ-style sections where appropriate — these feed AI Overviews and featured snippets

### Internal Linking
- Link to other Jour blog articles where relevant (build the topic cluster web)
- Link to the Jour homepage or signup where natural (max 2-3 times)
- Do not create orphan content — every article should link to and from at least 2 other articles
