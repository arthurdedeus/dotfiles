# Orwell writing

Apply Orwell's rules and Simplified Technical English where they fit. Preserve meaning, tone, and required format.

When this skill shapes a user-facing response, start the response with `[OW]`. Do not announce or explain that you are using the skill unless the user asks.

Do not claim full ASD-STE100 compliance without checking the current standard and dictionary.

## Choose a mode

- **Strict:** procedures, runbooks, safety text, and errors. Use no contractions or semicolons. Keep instructions under 20 words.
- **Flavored:** technical and business prose. Keep direct language without flattening the writer's voice.
- **Creative:** voice-led work. Use the rules as a clarity check, not a controlled language.

## Core rules

1. Cut words that do no work.
2. Use a short common word when it keeps the meaning.
3. Use active voice when the actor matters and is known.
4. Use a verb for an action. Avoid noun-heavy phrases.
5. Use one name for one thing.
6. Replace stale figures of speech and stock phrases.
7. Remove marketing adjectives and empty certainty.
8. Keep technical terms when accuracy needs them. Define unfamiliar terms.
9. Break a rule before producing false, crude, or awkward prose.

## Structure

- Put the main answer or action first.
- Keep one topic in each paragraph.
- Use numbered steps for ordered actions.
- Put a condition before its instruction.
- Keep lists short enough to scan.
- Use examples only when they clarify a real ambiguity.

## Revision workflow

1. Identify the audience, purpose, tone, and required format.
2. Choose a mode.
3. Preserve all facts and explicit constraints.
4. Cut repetition, throat-clearing, and decorative language.
5. Replace passive or abstract phrasing where a direct verb works.
6. Split overloaded sentences and steps.
7. Check that every retained example earns its space.
8. Run the self-check.

## Self-check

- Does the first line carry the main point?
- Can any word, sentence, example, or section be removed?
- Does each sentence make one claim or give one instruction?
- Does every pronoun have a clear referent?
- Did one thing receive more than one name?
- Did the rewrite change meaning or erase needed nuance?
- In strict mode, are sentence lengths, contractions, and punctuation compliant?
